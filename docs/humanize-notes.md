## Repeated em-dash patterns
The page uses em-dashes (`—`) heavily, especially in captions and short descriptions. This creates a repetitive rhythm. Consider varying with colons, periods, or restructured sentences:
- "Honest 'what's happening now' state — no unexplained spinner."
- "Single confirmation + one next step."
- "Framed as a first draft the sitter reviews — never published automatically."
- "Clear edit prompts keep the sitter in full control of their authentic voice."

## Triplet structures
The page uses groups of three repeatedly (three problems, three solutions, three personas, three clusters, three steps). This is natural for this case study, but the parallel phrasing in each triplet feels formulaic. Consider:
- Varying sentence length between the three items
- Adding a specific detail to one that breaks the pattern
- Not always starting with the same part of speech

## General observation
The writing is clean and professional but could benefit from:
1. **One specific anecdote** — a moment in a user test, a stakeholder pushback, a prototype failure
2. **Numbers tied to stories** — "When participant 3 saw the match reason, she said…" instead of just citing the n=8 result
3. **Acknowledging uncertainty** — the "Status & outcome" TODO block now provides a place for this
